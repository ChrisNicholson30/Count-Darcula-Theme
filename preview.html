The palette

Eight hues, one lightness

Colour carries meaning here, brightness never does. Every accent is generated from an OKLCH coordinate at the same L*, with per-hue chroma tuned so none of them looks louder than the others. Contrast is measured, not guessed.

The neutral ramp

The argument

Why it’s easier on the eyes

Both parents let their token colours drift across the lightness scale. The eye re-adapts at every jump, and after a few hours that costs you. Here is the same eight roles in all three themes, plotted by perceptual lightness — longer bar, brighter token.

01 One background

#282b35 is the literal perceptual midpoint of One Dark’s #282c34 and Dracula’s #282a36 — not a compromise, an average.

02 No pure white

The foreground stops at #d4d7e0 — bright enough to read at 9.8:1, dim enough not to bloom against a dark canvas at two in the morning.

03 Quiet punctuation

Commas, semicolons and accessors sit one step below the foreground. You stop reading the scaffolding and start reading the code.

Accessibility

Every text colour, measured

Checked against WCAG 2.1 contrast ratios and APCA Lc — which models light-on-dark text far better than WCAG 2.x does, and a dark theme is nothing but light-on-dark text. npm run audit fails the build if any colour slips below AA on the surface it actually renders on.

RoleHexOKLCHWCAGAPCA LcRating

Rated against the editor canvas for the selected variant. AAA is ≥ 7:1, AA is ≥ 4.5:1. Comments are deliberately kept above AA too — a comment is still text, and a comment you cannot read is a comment nobody maintains.

Battery

Nocturne turns most of the screen off

On an OLED laptop panel every subpixel makes its own light, so panel power follows the image. Nocturne paints every large surface pure black and steps the whole foreground down ~8 L*, which the model below puts at — less panel power than the flagship.

How this is estimated

Per-channel OLED power model (Dong, Choi & Zhong, DAC 2009), blue weighted 1.56× green because blue emitters are the least efficient, applied to a modelled full-screen VS Code window: 60% editor, side bar, terminal, chrome, and ~10% glyph coverage distributed across real token frequencies. Code in src/power.js — read it and disagree with it.

What it will not do

On a conventional LCD the backlight is on regardless of what you display, so a dark theme saves you essentially nothing directly. The honest win there is indirect: a dimmer image is comfortable at a lower brightness setting, and backlight power scales steeply with brightness.
